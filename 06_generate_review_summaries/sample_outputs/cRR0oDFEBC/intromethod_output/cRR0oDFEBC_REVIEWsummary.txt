HUMAN REVIEW SUMMARY
------------------------------------------------------------
The paper's focus on verifiable instructions inherently limits its generalization to open-ended or subjective instructions, raising concerns about its applicability to less structured or ambiguous tasks. Methodologically, the cross-verification approach in Section 3.2 is questioned for its reliance on test-function self-consistency, which may not be reliable if bad tests compromise correct functions or if both tests and functions are flawed, leading to corrupted data quality. The absence of a baseline where responses are validated through the LLM itself without code further obscures the value of the verification function and test cases. Additionally, the proposed workflow struggles to scale to complex instructions, particularly those that are semantically rich and cannot be evaluated via execution, and real-world scenarios requiring various dependencies and APIs pose scalability challenges. Clarity issues arise from the lack of examples on failed cases and the limited domain of seed instructions, which are confined to string manipulation and could benefit from diversification. The paper's heavy focus on execution feedback, without considering other methods like human feedback or model-based evaluation, suggests a lack of methodological diversity, although execution feedback and iterative optimization are noted to work well. The instruction-following evaluations on IFBench and FollowBench are criticized for lacking practicability, as they only contain quantifiable instructions like word counts, and more practical scenarios, such as those in BigCodeBench, should be considered. Finally, the current evaluation is limited to English-only, and assessing the models on multilingual setups would better demonstrate their generalizability.

LLM REVIEW SUMMARY
------------------------------------------------------------
The paper presents a methodology that includes instruction augmentation and verification, which is technically sound and well-structured as described in Sections 3.2 and 3.3. However, a significant issue arises from the absence of a methods section, making it impossible to evaluate the technical soundness or correctness of the methodology, as no directly relevant method section is found. Despite this, the methodology for using execution feedback and verification functions is technically sound and consistent with the claim, as detailed in Section 3.2. Yet, the absence of a methods section means there is no description of the methodology to verify if it is technically sound or capable of achieving the claimed accuracy. In terms of clarity, while the methodology is generally clear, the explanation of the back-translation verification process could be more detailed, as noted in Section 3.2. The lack of a methods section results in a lack of clarity and completeness in explaining how the claim is achieved. Additionally, the methods section lacks an explicit connection between execution feedback and its role as a reward model in DPO, which could lead to confusion. The missing methods section further leads to a lack of clarity and completeness in explaining how the claimed accuracy is achieved.
